Understanding Digital Inclusion in Australia

We are living in a digital era where internet access is essential for education, work, and daily life. Yet not all Australians are equally included. This project explores how household income and wealth affect digital inclusion, highlighting inequalities in access, affordability, and digital ability across states, territories, and income groups.

Australian Digital Inclusion Map

1. Digital Inclusion Across States

Not all Australians are equally connected to the digital world. This map shows that the Australian Capital Territory consistently leads in digital inclusion index, while Tasmania and the Northern Territory lag behind. Most regions are slowly improving, but persistent gaps highlight ongoing digital inequalities. Use the year slider to see how these differences have evolved over time.


Digital Inclusion Index (DGI)
A score combining access, affordability and digital ability

2. Digital Inclusion Across Income Groups

Income matters when it comes to digital inclusion. This heatmap reveals how access, affordability, and digital ability differ across income groups (Q1–Q5) over seven years, where Q1 represents the lowest-income households and Q5 the highest. Lower-income households face bigger challenges in accessing devices and internet, and in developing digital skills, while higher-income households enjoy more opportunities. Move through the years using the slider to see how gaps persist or narrow over time.

Digital Access Disparities by Income Quintile

3. Income Distribution per week by Quintile

How is income spread across Australian households? This treemap
shows the proportion of total income earned by each income quintile
per week. Larger rectangles represent higher shares, revealing the
stark differences between lower- and higher-income groups. Explore
the chart to see which groups dominate income distribution and which
are left behind.


4. Composition of Main Income Sources

Not all income is earned the same way. This doughnut chart breaks
down households’ main sources of income, such as employee income
or government transfers. Each slice shows the proportion of
households relying on each source, helping us understand the
diversity of income streams across Australia.

5. Comparing Digital Inclusion Across States

Some states are stronger than others in digital inclusion. This grouped
bar chart shows how each state performs in access, affordability, and
digital ability. Click on the legend to filter and compare states, or
focus on Victoria to see key scores highlighted. This makes it easy to
spot strengths, weaknesses, and opportunities for improvement across
the country. You can click on the legend to highlight certain states.


6. Australia’s Digital Inclusion Profile

Finally, let’s look at Australia as a whole. This radar chart
summarizes national performance across access, affordability,
and digital ability. It shows where Australians excel and where gaps
remain, providing a clear snapshot of the country’s digital inclusion
strengths and challenges. Use this overview to reflect on where efforts
could make the biggest impact.
